*To what extent should one trust a statement that a program is free of Trojan horses? Perhaps it is more important to trust the people who wrote the software.*

*— Ken Thompson, Reflections on Trusting Trust (1984)*

This quote is perhaps more relevant today than it was when it was written. Ken Thompson observed long before the current AI boom that trusting a piece of software necessitates trusting the person who wrote it. The fact that software performs its advertised function alone is not grounds for trust.

Trust is built—often over many years—through consistent performance and quality. It is supported by less tangible qualities such as the customer support experience, transparency, and respect among industry experts. Trust cannot be engineered; it must be earned.

Today, developers and users alike are asking the same question: How does the use of AI affect trust in software companies?

I think the answer is the same today as it was in 1984: trust the people, not the product. AI has given millions of people the ability to create software. It has not, however, created millions of software developers. As the volume of software grows, so too will the importance of knowing who is behind it. A track record of quality, transparency, and accountability matter far more than the tools used to build it.
